# The stopped clock: a periodic world cannot tell a specialist from a generalist

- **Entry:** `2026-08-22-1830-stopped-clock`
- **When:** 2026-08-22 18:11–18:45 CDT
- **Who:** Javid (observing his own run of the demo) + Claude
- **Experiment:** [003](../../experiments/003-transfer-retention/design.md), via the rule-flip demo
- **Code state:** git `9a9cbcb` + this commit
- **Re-run:** the [demo](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4751e69e-739c-459f-814c-2669a3229349), auto-flip 2,500, both rule modes

## In plain words

Javid ran the rule-flip demo longer than any verification run had (23 flips)
and pasted his scoreboard **[J]**. It showed the count arm — 100% frozen, 8%
plasticity, provably incapable of learning — "recovering" from 11 of 23 flips
with a median recovery of **102 trials**, and near-parity with α/β on every
behavioural measure: lifetime 0.526 vs 0.537, share-of-life-at-criterion 44%
vs 41%. His reading: *"They're both equally bad. They've learned one game, but
not the other."*

Both halves of that are right, and each exposes something different.

**The 102 is a stopped clock.** 102 is the metric's measurement floor (the
rolling-100 window has to fill before recovery is checkable — the L-032
artifact, third appearance today). A "recovery" at the floor means the arm was
at criterion the instant we could look: it learned nothing. The demo's world
alternates between two rules, the frozen arm froze while competent at one of
them, and every second flip the world swings back to its corpse. 11 of 23 ≈
every other flip. Rigidity farms a returning world.

**And the near-parity is real, not an artifact.** In a two-state world at this
stage budget, a frozen specialist and a flexible relearner genuinely tie on
outcomes. Only the mechanism gauges (100% vs 0% frozen) separate them —
behaviour does not, because the environment never demands what only the
flexible one can do.

So the world was made more demanding. A **"never looks back"** mode was added:
each flip draws a genuinely novel rule. Pre-stated prediction: count collapses
(a stopped clock is worthless in a world that never returns) and α/β keeps
relearning. Measured, 11 novel rules: count came back from **1 of 11** — and
**α/β came back from 1 of 11 too** (lifetime 0.428 vs 0.439; at-criterion 8%
vs 12%; plasticity 18% vs 52%). The prediction was half wrong, and the honest
half matters more: **flexible synapses are not yet flexible behaviour.** The
α/β model keeps every synapse movable, but relearning still costs 600–2,500
trials per rule — the learning rule's own slowness (L-030), not consolidation
— and a world of ceaseless novelty outruns it.

## Learnings

- **L-038:** **A periodic environment cannot distinguish a specialist from a
  generalist, and a returning rule lets rigidity mimic adaptation.** A 100%
  frozen organism "recovered" from 11 of 23 alternating reversals at the
  measurement floor — at criterion the moment the window filled, no weights
  moved — because the world kept swinging back to the mapping it froze into.
  Any flexibility metric must (a) separate *re-alignment* from *re-learning*
  (a recovery at the window floor is the former), and (b) include task
  sequences that never revisit a previous state. *Evidence:* Javid's 23-flip
  run **[J]**; the alternate-mode drive where early-life count recoveries were
  all genuine (median 495) shows the stopped clock is an emergent late-life
  strategy, not a constant.
- **L-039:** **Synaptic flexibility is not behavioural flexibility.** Under
  never-returning rules, α/β keeps 52% plasticity and 0% frozen yet comes back
  from 1 of 11 novel rules at a 2,500-trial budget — statistically tied with
  the frozen count arm on outcomes. The α/β fix removed the *capacity*
  bottleneck (L-036); the *speed* bottleneck is the rule's noisy credit
  assignment under contradiction (L-030), and it now binds first. *Evidence:*
  this entry's shuffle-mode drive.

## Decisions

1. **The demo's scoreboard now splits "came back" into *already aligned*
   (≤110 trials — the floor) and *genuinely relearned***, with the median
   reported over genuine relearning only, and carries the shuffle finding in
   its caption.
2. **Backlog track H gains its third hard requirement:** retention/flexibility
   scoring must use task sequences that do not revisit prior states (or score
   revisits separately), and must not credit floor-speed "recoveries" —
   otherwise a rigid entrant farms the leaderboard by standing still.
3. **The next lever for 003's line of work is the learning rule's reversal
   speed, not consolidation** — reaffirming the α/β entry's decision 3 with
   behavioural evidence.

## Threats to validity

1. Single seed per drive; per-stage variance is large.
2. The ≤110 instant threshold is one choice; it is the window floor plus frame
   granularity, but a fast genuine relearner below 110 would be misfiled as
   aligned (none has been observed — measured relearning runs 600+).
3. The shuffle drive is 11 flips; "both collapse" at other budgets is
   unmeasured (at, say, 5,000-trial stages α/β should separate — untested).
